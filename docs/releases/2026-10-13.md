# Part 1: For the clients

## Alerts

- **The bell clears itself.** An alert closes when its work is done, wherever
  it was done: call the new lead from Today and its "New lead" alert is gone.
  Moved to someone else, it leaves your bell. Twelve leads in one batch close
  when the last is worked. On the first day, the old piles go: Bhumi's agents
  from 143–300 unread each to 1–4; Mahalaxmi's from 150–188 to 4–11.
- **Tapping a phone notification counts as reading it**, and tapping one alert
  in the bell reads only that one.
- **Nothing buzzes outside the firm's working hours** — 9 am to 9 pm unless the
  owner changes it in Settings → Response times. At the opening hour each person
  gets one notification for whatever is still open.
- **Work handed to you reaches the phone straight away** — a new lead, a lead
  or owner moved to you (by name, when it is one), a lead that came back — and
  so do due follow-ups, even when an Android phone is idle. Tenancies ending and
  leads going untouched wait for the morning summary. A remark never buzzes.
- **A phone that is signed out stops getting alerts.** It used to keep
  receiving lead names while its owner was signed in somewhere else.
- **Team shows who alerts cannot reach**: an Alerts column — On, Off, Blocked,
  Browser cannot — with "N of M shown this week". This is how to see why Anil
  and Vinod get nothing, once each has opened the app after the update.

## Performance and My work

- **A week, a month, or any dates.** Beside the date: Day · 7 days · 30 days ·
  Custom. Every number, and the list it opens, covers those days. An agent
  opened from the month opens on the month.

# Part 2: For us

## What the API deploy does to the database

On its first start (`initSchema`), automatically:

- **Adds** columns to `notifications` (`read_at`, `record_kind`, `record_ids`,
  `subject_at`, `shown_at`, `closed_at`, `close_reason`, `urgency`,
  `send_after`, `pushed_at`) and two indexes; `push_subscriptions.session_id`;
  table `push_gone_endpoints`; `sessions.push_state`, `push_state_at`;
  `push_deliveries.notification_ids`, `payload`, `attempt`, `retried_at`.
- **Writes, once** (`2026_10_12_alert_lifecycle_first_run`): closes alerts whose
  work is already done. **Status columns on `notifications` only — bhumi and
  mahalaxmi included.** Previewed read-only on production, 13 Oct:

  | | open now | closes |
  |---|---|---|
  | bhumi | 2,246 | 2,227 |
  | mahalaxmi | 786 | 757 |

  The boot log prints `[Alerts] first run: …` with the real counts.

No new environment variables.

## Order

1. Check on `development` (dev API on EC2 + a dev frontend).
2. Merge `development` → `main` (a fast-forward; `main` has nothing of its own).
3. **API first**: `./scripts/deploy-api.sh` on the box. If days have passed,
   re-run the preview first:
   `npx tsx backend/src/scripts/alert-first-run-preview.ts --tenant=bhumi,mahalaxmi`
4. **Then the frontend** (Vercel). The other way round, Performance's ranges
   read as one day and Team's Alerts column shows "—" until the API catches up.

## What to check on development

- Bell: an alert for a lead you then call disappears within a few seconds;
  one tap in the drawer reads one alert.
- Team: the Alerts column shows a value per person.
- Performance: 30 days shows the month; a number opens the month's list.
- Superadmin → a firm → **Alerts**: the table renders.

## What to check on production, after

- Boot log first-run line ≈ the table above.
- Bells: agents at single digits.
- Apple push failures (110 of 458 in 14 days before) drop toward zero.
- The first 9 am summary goes out the next morning.
- After a week: console → Alerts says which alert types change behaviour.
